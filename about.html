About Appre

A resume can undersell you when it doesn't speak to what a specific employer asks for. Appre helps make that connection without exaggerating your experience: a person reviews your materials against the posting and records what matches and what doesn't.

Who does the work

Appre is run by a small team with backgrounds in technical recruiting and corporate HR. We started it after seeing two recurring problems: qualified applicants overlooked because their resumes didn't reflect the posting, and resumes stretched to compensate. Our approach addresses both: make relevant experience easier to see and be clear about genuine gaps.

The approach works across industries because it starts with the specific posting, not a one-size-fits-all resume formula. See it applied across nine fields in our case studies.

What "honest" means in practice

It's easy to say a service is honest. It's more useful to show what that costs us. Three concrete commitments:

  • We will not invent a credential, certification, or years of experience you don't have, even worded carefully. If a posting requires something you lack, the gap-check report says so — see the example below.
  • We will not quietly drop a "preferred" gap into obscurity by omission. If we leave something off the resume because it's not relevant, we tell you why, not just that it's gone.
  • If, after a gap check, we think a posting is a poor match for your background, we'll say that too — even though it means less work for us, not more.

A concrete example of the commitment

This is the kind of note that actually goes into a gap-check report — not a paraphrase, the real shape of the language.

jd"Bachelor's degree in Computer Science or related field required."
−Candidate has an Associate's degree and 6 years of relevant self-taught and on-the-job experience, no bachelor's degree. This is a real gap against a stated requirement, not a preference. We will not describe an Associate's degree as a Bachelor's degree, and we will not omit education entirely to avoid the question — the resume lists the degree actually held.
+Recommendation given to candidate: some employers apply "or equivalent experience" informally even when not written; worth applying if the rest of the match is strong, with the resume's experience section doing the work the degree line can't. Final call is the candidate's, made with accurate information.

What we don't claim

No interview-rate percentage, no "guaranteed" outcome, no claim about how many people we've worked with. Those numbers are common in this industry and we don't think most of them are verifiable by the people reading them, which is the same reason we don't use them here. What we do put in writing: the process on how it works, the packages on pricing, and the honesty commitment above — each of those is something you can hold us to.
