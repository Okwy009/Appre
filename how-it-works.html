How it works

We review a real job posting, connect it to your experience, and show you both the matches and the gaps. Here's what happens from first review to finished application materials.

  1. 1

    Start with the job posting

    Send the job description you're applying to, or a few postings for similar roles. Titles alone don't show which skills, experience, or qualifications each employer is asking for.

  2. 2

    We turn it into a clear checklist

    We identify required and preferred qualifications, named tools and certifications, and the words the employer uses to describe the work. That gives us a consistent way to compare the posting with your experience.

  3. 3

    We make relevant experience easier to recognize

    When your background supports a requirement, we describe that experience using the posting's terms. We don't add skills or claims you can't support; the aim is to make the connection clear, not to make your experience sound like something it isn't.

  4. 4

    We show you what doesn't line up

    We flag requirements your background doesn't clearly meet, including required credentials, specific tools, and related-but-not-identical experience. We explain which gaps matter and discuss honest options, such as describing adjacent experience, addressing a gap in a cover letter, or focusing on a better-fit role.

  5. 5

    Review your draft and get your files

    You receive a draft resume, gap-check notes, and a cover letter if it's included in your package. We revise based on your feedback, then deliver an ATS-readable document and a PDF. See pricing for what's included in each package.

Here's what a gap check looks like

Each note compares a specific job requirement with the experience you shared.

jd"5+ years managing a direct-report team, including at least one budget cycle."
+Match — 6 years managing teams of 4–9, ran two annual budget cycles at [role]. Mirrored directly.
jd"PMP certification required."
−Gap — no PMP on file. This is listed as required, not preferred. Options: note it's in progress if that's true, apply anyway if your experience is otherwise strong and the market for this role is flexible, or treat this as a signal to prioritize other postings.

We don't reword "no certification" into something that sounds like a certification. That's the commitment described in full on the about page.
